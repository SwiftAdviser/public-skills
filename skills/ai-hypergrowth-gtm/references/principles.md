# Principles

This is a compact, public-safe reference. For full detail, use the original research pages linked in `source-index.md`.

## Growth Principles

### Wedge That Prints Value

Launch around one painful workflow with clear economic value. The wedge must be specific enough to prove in the customer's environment and big enough to support serious revenue before the company expands into a platform narrative.

Anti-pattern: sell "AI platform for X" before proving one workflow.

### Prestige-First Beachhead

If the market follows elite buyers, the first logos matter more than easy mid-market revenue. Anchor on the buyer whose trust causes others to move.

Anti-pattern: assume a mid-market motion will naturally move upmarket.

### Domain-Expert GTM

The best early sellers often come from the buyer's world: lawyers, clinicians, operators, revenue leaders, support leaders, engineers, or domain practitioners. They sell with peer credibility and know what proof matters.

Anti-pattern: hire generic AEs before the domain-specific objection map is clear.

### Proof Before Scale

Build proof before scaling outbound. Strong proof has a baseline, a named workflow, a timebox, a primary metric, and a conversion path. Paid design partners beat free, vague pilots because payment filters urgency.

Anti-pattern: free trials with no metric, no baseline, and no full-contract terms.

### Labor-Cost Pricing

Price against the work replaced or compressed, not against adjacent SaaS tools. Anchor to hours, headcount, agency spend, cost per ticket, cost per review, cost per resolved case, or cost of delay.

Anti-pattern: compare against cheaper software when the real value is avoiding expensive human work.

### Product-Led Expansion Logic

Expansion should be built into usage. More cases, more departments, more workflows, more data, or more outcomes should naturally expand revenue.

Anti-pattern: every expansion depends on a separate heroic sales motion.

## Sales Principles

### Founder-Led Until Proven

Founders must run early deals until ICP, pricing, demo, objections, pilot terms, and success metrics are validated. A VP Sales should scale a known motion, not discover it.

### Demo on the Prospect's Work

Use the prospect's own artifacts: documents, tickets, code, filings, calls, docs, community logs, workflows, or public footprint. This removes imagination from the sales process.

### WTP Over Enthusiasm

Ask directly what they would pay. Treat low willingness to pay as a signal about buyer, pain, or product, not a negotiation problem.

### Paid Pilots With Exit Conditions

A paid pilot should have one workflow, one owner, one baseline, one primary success metric, one timebox, and full-contract pricing agreed before kickoff.

### Objector as Champion

The strongest objector may have the most at stake. If proof converts them, they can become the strongest internal advocate because they carry operational authority.

### Practitioner Pull

Solve personal pain for practitioners first when they can create internal pull. Then let practitioner dependency move the buyer toward procurement and budget owners.
